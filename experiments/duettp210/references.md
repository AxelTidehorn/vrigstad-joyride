# Current reference scope — P210 EPA final correction

## Original P210 front and cabin

[1966 Volvo P210 Duett B18, Bilweb Auctions](https://www.bilwebauctions.se/juliauktion-3-2026/volvo-p210-duett-b18-55071). The near-side image, front/rear three-quarter images and front image were downloaded and visually inspected. The near-side view materially improves on the perspective-only evidence used previously. It is still a photograph, not an orthographic drawing. The sale describes the car as a 1966 P210; original bonnet, front wings, windshield, roof cap, door and rear opening provide shape evidence. Its estate rear volume is not copied as the EPA bed.

`final-pass/profile-analysis.json` records the exact original image URL, manually identified wheel centres and control points, common wheelbase scaling, estimated coordinates and uncertainties. Photos remain outside the repository and are linked, not redistributed.

[Volvo Cars P210 Duett history](https://www.volvocars.com/intl/cars/legacy-models/p210-duett/): manufacturer confirms **2600 mm wheelbase**, P210 production 1960–1969 and curved windscreen introduced with P210. This is the verified dimensional anchor. Engine/transmission specifications are not implemented in this modelling task.

## One consistent EPA conversion

* [Duett a-traktor 1.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Duett_a-traktor_1.jpg), Fmmattsson, 9 December 2014: orange TELE conversion, front view.
* [Duett a-traktor 2.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Duett_a-traktor_2.jpg), same photographer/date and matching orange TELE build: rear/near-side three-quarter.

This build supplies the cut high rounded cab and a bed based on retained wide quarter panels, not independent round hot-rod mudguards. Modern wheel choices, decoration, exhaust and other accessories are excluded. This is a stylised interpretation, not a legal EPA/A-traktor or exact registration/year certification. The conversion dimensions cannot be verified from these perspective views.

The black Hova EPA photographed by Liftarn was the earlier primary reference. It remains historical research but no longer defines this pass's rear panels. That avoids mixing its free-standing mudguard scheme into the chosen retained-quarter conversion.
